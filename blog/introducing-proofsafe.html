Introducing ProofSafe QA: validation evidence you can inspect

Project launch · 25 November 2026 · AI-assisted, human-operated

ProofSafe QA is a small open-source browser application built for the KaneCLI Online Hackathon. It explores a practical question: when an AI-assisted tool says that validation passed, what evidence can a developer actually inspect?

The application validates simple invoice-like CSV records locally. It flags duplicate identifiers and invalid amounts, offers a synthetic failing example, and exports a JSON report. The report explicitly separates local browser validation from Kane verification, official regulatory approval and payment settlement. It does not upload customer CSV records.

The current implementation is deliberately small. It has ten deterministic unit tests and an independent Chromium acceptance suite. It is not a full CSV parser or production invoice compliance engine; quoted commas and multiline records are outside the prototype’s scope.

KaneCLI was used for two genuine browser-test runs against the public deployment. The first result informed an AI-assisted change, and the second checked the updated application. See the companion engineering retrospective for the precise assertions and the limitations of those checks.
